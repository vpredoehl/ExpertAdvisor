GOAL

Implement the advisory ranking, comparison, and presentation layer over persisted Phase 4B Step 1 evaluation evidence.

This step must answer:

1. Which evaluated recommendations rank highest within a defined advisory scope?
2. Why does one recommendation rank above another?
3. Which recommendations are comparable?
4. Which recommendations are blocked, stale, duplicate, or incomplete?
5. What evidence and score components explain rank differences?
6. Can the ranking be reproduced exactly from persisted Step 1 evidence?
7. Can users inspect rankings without recalculating source experiment evidence?
8. Can users compare two or more recommendations deterministically?
9. Can ranking snapshots remain auditable when later evaluations or policies change?
10. Can the same ranking request converge idempotently under concurrency?

REASONING

Phase 4B Step 1 created immutable, deterministic advisory evaluation evidence. Step 2 should not reopen source experiment evidence or invent a second score. Its role is to organize persisted evaluations into reproducible advisory views, make ordering transparent, and explain why recommendations differ.

Persisted ranking snapshots are necessary because a query over current evaluations is not a durable historical decision artifact. Exact membership, scope, policy, bucket, tie-break values, and rank must remain inspectable after later evaluations and policy versions are added.

Comparison must also be policy-aware. Recommendations evaluated under incompatible scoring or evaluator versions cannot be presented as directly better or worse without an explicit compatibility rule. Step 2 therefore introduces deterministic comparability states and component-level differences rather than fabricated conclusions.

This increment remains advisory. It improves research visibility and decision support while preserving the separation between evaluation, ranking, approval, experiment creation, queueing, profitability analysis, and scheduler execution.
