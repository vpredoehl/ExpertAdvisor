BLOCKING ARCHITECTURAL CORRECTIONS

1. Separate lifecycle, consistency, and scientific comparison.

Represent terminal/lifecycle state explicitly, including at minimum:

- not_terminal
- succeeded
- failed
- cancelled
- unknown

Represent consistency independently:

- consistent
- inconsistent

Preserve deterministic diagnostic codes for inconsistent downstream evidence.

Do not use MissingResultEvidence as a substitute for lifecycle state.

Derive explicit member-level outcomes sufficient to distinguish, at minimum:

- not ready / nonterminal
- succeeded and comparable
- succeeded with context change
- succeeded with metric gap
- terminal failed
- terminal cancelled
- inconsistent

Names may differ if the resulting contract is clearer and internally consistent.

2. Replace singular metrics with deterministic metric collections.

Source evidence and result evidence must support multiple scientific metrics for the same campaign member.

Requirements:

- one member can carry inference accuracy and leader score simultaneously;
- future metrics can be added without redesigning member cardinality;
- reject duplicate metric identities within one evidence collection;
- canonicalize and order metrics deterministically by metric identity;
- derive one comparison per metric;
- keep member outcome counts separate from per-metric comparison and delta counts;
- metric input order must not affect identity or output ordering.

3. Make successful-result payload conditional on lifecycle.

Failed, cancelled, inconsistent, proposal-only, and otherwise nonterminal members must not require fabricated:

- experiment IDs
- model IDs
- result IDs
- context
- metric values

A proposal-only member must be representable as nonterminal/not ready rather than being mislabeled MissingResultEvidence.

4. Add exact result provenance validation.

Bind result evidence to the exact materialized campaign member and its expected downstream experiment identity.

Requirements:

- validate result experiment identity against the member's expected experiment evidence when present;
- reject cross-member reuse of the same result experiment or result identity;
- reject provenance mismatches deterministically;
- preserve malformed downstream evidence as an inconsistent member with diagnostics when exact top-level materialization membership remains trustworthy;
- only fail the entire assessment when the materialization/member identity itself cannot be trusted.

5. Protect output invariants.

Derived member and aggregate outputs must be valid by construction or completely validated at construction.

Prevent callers from creating contradictory combinations of:

- lifecycle
- consistency
- diagnostics
- comparisons
- summaries
- counts
- canonical identities

Restrict constructors/factories as appropriate, or perform complete cross-field validation.

6. Preserve Step 5a scope and existing strengths.

Retain:

- immutable output objects;
- finite-number validation;
- positive-ID validation where IDs are present and required;
- contiguous ordinal validation;
- uniqueness validation;
- deterministic member ordering;
- deterministic diagnostic ordering;
- deterministic metric/comparison ordering;
- locale-independent canonicalization and hashing;
- observed_at excluded from evidence identity;
- no external I/O or persistence.
